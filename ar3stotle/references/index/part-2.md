# Structure index, part 2 of 4 (ids 251-500)

One per line: `id | name | tradition | what it carries`. Read for emotional register, not format keywords.

251 | Revenge Tragedy | Universal | Renaissance English subgenre. Protagonist seeks revenge for kinsman's murder, undergoes moral degradation, dies with antagonists.
252 | Domestic Tragedy | Universal | Tragedy set in middle- or working-class domestic life rather than royal settings. Democratization of tragic stature.
253 | Comedy of Manners | Universal | Satirical comedy that mocks the affectations, etiquette, and pretensions of a specific social class. The gap between manners and reality.
254 | Farce | Universal | Comedy built on improbable situations, mistaken identities, slamming doors, and accelerating chaos. Physical and situational comedy as escalating impossibility.
255 | Satire | Universal | Comedy with critical intent — exposing folly, hypocrisy, or vice through ridicule. Two main modes: Horatian (gentle) and Juvenalian (savage).
256 | Black / Dark Comedy | Universal | Comedy that finds humor in subjects normally treated with seriousness or horror — death, disease, war, atrocity. Uncomfortable laughter; grim reality is funny.
257 | Slapstick | Universal | Physical comedy based on broad, exaggerated, body-based humor — pratfalls, pies in faces, chase sequences. Pure body-driven, language-free comedy.
258 | Parody | Universal | Comedy that imitates the style and conventions of a specific work or genre to comic effect. The formal target is what's being mocked.
259 | Opera narrative | Universal | Sung-through dramatic narrative combining music, poetry, and theater. 3-4 acts with arias for character interiority, ensembles for conflict.
260 | Broadway Musical | Universal | American musical-theater form combining book (spoken scenes), songs, and dance. English vernacular and integrated book-songs structure.
261 | Ballet narrative | Universal | Narrative dance work, typically without text, communicating story through choreography, music, costume, and staging. Body-narrative — story through bodies in motion.
262 | Testimonial Ad | Universal | Advertising in which a real (or plausible) customer narrates their experience with the product. Peer credibility rather than authority endorsement.
263 | Comparison Ad | Universal | Advertising that explicitly compares the product to a competitor's, highlighting advantages. Named adversarial structure.
264 | Lifestyle Ad | Universal | Advertising that sells the product through association with an aspirational way of life rather than functional features. Identity-coded value.
265 | Before / After Ad | Universal | Advertising that shows the subject's state before using the product and after, with the product as transformative agent. Direct, conversion-focused.
266 | Product Launch Story | Universal | Narrative announcing a new product, woven around the problem the product solves, the insight that produced it, and the future it makes possible.
267 | Filibuster narrative | Universal | Extended speech delivered to delay legislative action — the act of speaking is itself the political move. Hybrid: substantive content + procedural function.
268 | Town Hall Address | Universal | Politician's direct engagement with constituents, structured as remarks followed by Q&A. Semi-improvised middle and accountability-under-pressure tenor.
269 | Debate format | Universal | Structured public confrontation between candidates or positions, governed by formal rules of time, response, and rebuttal. Contest visible to voters.
270 | Pilgrimage Memoir | Universal | First-person narrative of a religious or spiritual pilgrimage — Mecca, Jerusalem, Camino, Varanasi, Mount Kailash. The journey is interior as well as exterior.
271 | Spiritual Autobiography | Universal | Long-form first-person account of a life's spiritual development. Distinct from secular autobiography by emphasis on the soul's journey.
272 | Allegorical Religious Narrative | Universal | Narrative whose surface story figures a deeper spiritual journey — characters, places, and events stand for spiritual states or lessons. Spiritual content encountered through story rather than argument.
273 | Lecture | Universal | Structured oral presentation of a topic by an expert to learners, typically 30-60 minutes. Pedagogical (not peer-review) audience.
274 | Tutorial / How-To Lesson | Universal | Step-by-step instructional content designed to teach a specific skill or process. Operational aim: learner can DO something at the end.
275 | Textbook Chapter | Universal | Structured educational chapter combining exposition, examples, and review questions. Engineered for systematic curriculum coverage.
276 | TED Talk format | Universal | Short (5-18 minute) talk format engineered for memorability and viral sharing. Combines personal story, big idea, and call to action.
277 | Khan Academy / Video Tutorial | Universal | Short, screen-captured video lesson explaining a single concept with examples — common in modern online education. Specific compressed-video format.
278 | MOOC Module | Universal | Self-paced online course unit combining video lecture, readings, quizzes, and discussion. Asynchronous remote-pedagogy structure.
279 | Coaching / Mentorship Conversation | Universal | Structured developmental conversation between mentor and mentee — questions, reflection, accountability. Specific developmental-relationship register.
280 | Apprenticeship / Master Class | Universal | Direct skill-transmission from master practitioner to learner — through demonstration, critique, and supervised practice. Embodied, master-led register.
281 | Hard Science Fiction | Universal | SF grounded in scientifically plausible extrapolation — physics, chemistry, biology as known are respected. The pleasure is in rigorous extrapolation.
282 | Space Opera | Universal | Large-scale science fiction featuring interstellar adventure, romance, and conflict — often soft on scientific plausibility, strong on adventure. Epic scale.
283 | Time Travel narrative | Universal | Narrative in which characters travel between time periods. Sub-types: closed-loop, branching, free-will.
284 | First Contact narrative | Universal | Narrative centered on humanity's first encounter with an alien intelligence. Core question: understanding across radical difference.
285 | Dystopian narrative | Universal | Narrative set in future or alternate society organized around oppressive principles — typically projected from author's present. Warning through extrapolation.
286 | Utopian narrative | Universal | Narrative depicting an idealized society — explicitly imagined as better than the present. Less narratively dramatic than dystopian; more politically prescriptive.
287 | Post-apocalyptic narrative | Universal | Narrative set after civilizational collapse — nuclear, viral, environmental, supernatural. What survives, what is rebuilt, what is lost.
288 | Alternate History | Universal | Narrative set in a world where history diverged at a specific point — Hitler won, the Confederacy survived, the Roman Empire didn't fall. Counterfactual exploration of consequence.
289 | Cyberpunk | Universal | Near-future SF combining "high tech and low life" — corporate-dominated dystopias, pervasive networked technology, hardboiled outsider protagonists. Established literary genre since 1984.
290 | Steampunk | Universal | Speculative fiction set in alternate Victorian-era past where steam-power technology has advanced — airships, mechanical computers, brass-and-leather aesthetics. Historical-fantasy hybrid.
291 | Solarpunk | Universal | Speculative fiction depicting hopeful, ecologically-integrated futures — renewable energy, biomimetic architecture, post-scarcity local economies. Counter-genre to dystopia and cyberpunk pessimism.
292 | Climate Fiction (Cli-Fi) | Universal | Fiction grappling explicitly with climate change — its causes, ongoing consequences, possible futures. Spans dystopia, near-future realism, post-apocalyptic.
293 | Military Science Fiction | Universal | SF foregrounding military structure, combat, and command — often in interstellar conflicts. Chain-of-command, tactics, experience of soldiering.
294 | Generation Ship narrative | Universal | SF set aboard slower-than-light spaceship whose journey takes generations. Generational drift and societal evolution within the ship are core content.
295 | AI / Robot Narrative | Universal | SF featuring artificial intelligence or robots as protagonists or central agents — exploring consciousness, autonomy, and the line between human and machine.
296 | Singularity narrative | Universal | SF set around the moment when AI surpasses human cognition — the technological singularity. Focus on the transformation event itself and its civilizational consequences.
297 | Mind Upload narrative | Universal | SF in which consciousness is digitized, transferred, or copied — exploring identity, mortality, and the meaning of self when continuity is decoupled from a single body.
298 | Virtual Reality narrative | Universal | SF set within or around immersive virtual environments — the relationship between real and simulated reality is central. Sub-form of cyberpunk but distinct.
299 | Apocalypse-in-Progress (slow apocalypse) | Universal | SF depicting civilization's collapse not as cataclysm but as gradual unraveling — degradation, abandonment, slow decay observed across time. Mid-apocalyptic rather than post-apocalyptic.
300 | Time Loop | Universal | Narrative in which the protagonist relives the same period of time repeatedly — usually until they figure out how to escape. Repetition itself as structural device.
301 | Alien Invasion narrative | Universal | SF in which Earth is attacked by extraterrestrial forces; humanity must resist, survive, or comprehend the invader. Distinguished from First Contact by its hostile-encounter premise.
302 | Pandemic / Outbreak narrative | Universal | Narrative of a disease outbreak — its origin, spread, response, and consequences. Spans medical thriller, science fiction, and post-apocalyptic.
303 | Dystopian YA | Universal | Young-adult fiction set in dystopian futures, with adolescent protagonists. YA conventions: chosen-one, romantic triangle, clear good-and-evil.
304 | Robinsonade (castaway narrative) | Universal | Narrative of person stranded in isolation — typically on an island — who must survive and create order from scratch. Civilization-building from nothing.
305 | Lost World narrative | Universal | Adventure fiction in which an isolated location preserves an ancient or otherwise lost ecosystem — dinosaurs, prehistoric peoples, forgotten civilizations.
306 | Dieselpunk | Universal | Speculative fiction set in alternate 1920s-1950s timelines with diesel-era technology aesthetics — propeller-driven aircraft, art deco, two-stroke industrial.
307 | Slipstream / Weird Fiction | Universal | Fiction that exists between genres — neither fully realist, fully fantastic, nor fully horror — using estrangement and dream-logic. Refusal of clean genre placement.
308 | New Weird | Universal | Late-20th/early-21st-c. literary movement combining weird fiction, fantasy, horror, SF — often urban-decay settings, biological-grotesque imagery, political weight.
309 | Hopepunk | Universal | Reaction against grimdark — fiction insisting that hope, kindness, and small acts of decency are forms of rebellion against darkness. Defiance through goodness.
310 | Generation-Skip Future History | Universal | SF that traces a civilization across centuries, jumping between generations — future-history form. Planetary or interstellar civilization, not single ship.
311 | Anthropological SF | Universal | SF whose primary interest is the imagined social structure, language, and cosmology of a fictional society — often through visitor-anthropologist protagonist.
312 | Hard Cyberpunk / Post-cyberpunk | Universal | Cyberpunk that takes the sociology and politics of network society as central — less neon-noir, more institutional analysis. Surveillance, capital, labor.
313 | Wuxia-Influenced Fantasy (Western adaptation) | Universal | Western fantasy drawing structural elements from Chinese wuxia — martial-honor codes, qi-energy systems, jianghu-like underworlds. Distinct from native wuxia by being non-Chinese-cultural.
314 | LitRPG (Literary Role-Playing Game) | Universal | Speculative fiction that incorporates explicit RPG mechanics — leveling up, stats, classes — into the narrative. Diegetic game-system.
315 | Police Procedural | Universal | Crime narrative following methodical investigation by police — emphasizing routines, hierarchies, and frustrations of professional detective work. Institutional, multi-actor focus.
316 | Locked Room Mystery | Universal | Mystery in which a crime appears physically impossible — committed in a sealed environment with no apparent way for the perpetrator to enter or exit. The puzzle is the mechanism.
317 | Howcatchem (Inverted Detective Story) | Universal | Inverted from whodunit — audience knows from beginning who committed the crime, and the pleasure is watching the detective methodically catch the criminal.
318 | Forensic Procedural | Universal | Crime narrative centered on forensic science — DNA, ballistics, autopsy, toxicology — as the detective's primary tool. Foregrounds the laboratory.
319 | Cold Case Narrative | Universal | Old, unsolved crime is reopened — typically because of new evidence, new technology, or persistent investigators. Archaeology-of-crime register.
320 | True Crime narrative | Universal | Non-fiction account of an actual crime, typically a murder, told with novelistic technique. Distinct from journalism by literary scope; from fiction by documentary grounding.
321 | Caper / Comic Heist | Universal | Lighter, comedic variant of the heist. The team is more eccentric, stakes more whimsical, tone playful.
322 | Noir | Universal | Crime narrative steeped in moral ambiguity, urban decay, fatalism, shadowed visual register. Pervasive philosophical despair — protagonist is doomed, system corrupt.
323 | Scandinavian Noir (Nordic Noir) | Universal | Crime fiction from Nordic countries combining bleak landscapes, social-democratic critique, morally complex investigators. Sociological depth — crime as symptom of societal disease.
324 | Tartan Noir | Universal | Scottish crime fiction characterized by working-class urban settings (Glasgow, Edinburgh), dark humor, political-social grit. Crime, place-specificity, and Celtic darkness.
325 | Conspiracy Thriller | Universal | Narrative in which protagonist gradually uncovers a vast hidden plot — typically governmental, corporate, or institutional — that threatens to destroy them. Paranoia-driven.
326 | Political Thriller | Universal | Thriller built around governmental power — assassination plots, electoral conspiracies, succession crises. Domestic-political setting.
327 | Domestic Thriller | Universal | Thriller set within a marriage, family, or household — the menace comes from intimate proximity. Claustrophobic setting; betrayal among supposed loved ones.
328 | Psychological Thriller | Universal | Thriller in which the engine is the protagonist's psychological state — paranoia, gaslighting, unreliable perception. Threat is partly internal; reader cannot trust the viewpoint.
329 | Serial Killer Thriller | Universal | Thriller centered on the pursuit of a serial murderer — often paired with psychologically scarred investigator who must enter the killer's mind. Ritual-of-killing patterns and forensic-psychological investigation.
330 | Whydunit | Universal | Mystery in which the criminal's identity is known or revealed early; the question and investigation focus on why — motive, history, psychology. Emphasis on understanding rather than capture.
331 | Locked Room Heist | Universal | Heist set within a single, enclosed location — bank vault, museum, casino. Compressed setting; the team must operate within tight spatial and temporal constraints.
332 | Procedural Drama (general) | Universal | Episodic drama centered on the routines of a profession (medical, legal, police, scientific) where each episode features a self-contained case while a serialized arc plays out across episodes.
333 | Medical Mystery | Universal | Mystery in which the puzzle is a baffling medical condition — a rare disease, an unexplained outbreak, an undiagnosed patient. The detective is a doctor; the criminal is biology.
334 | Treasure Hunt | Universal | Quest narrative organized around a hidden valuable — gold, artifact, document — guarded by clues, traps, and rivals. Cipher-puzzle mechanics.
335 | Spy Procedural / Tradecraft Narrative | Universal | Spy narrative emphasizing the bureaucratic, methodical work of intelligence services — agent runners, analysts, debriefings — over Bond-style action. Realism and institutional texture.
336 | Conspiracy of Silence narrative | Universal | A small community or institution maintains a shared silence around an old wrong — death, injustice, secret — and the protagonist's investigation threatens to break the silence.
337 | Detective Memoir | Universal | First-person narrative of a real or fictional detective's career, structured as remembered cases. Retrospective autobiographical voice.
338 | Heist-Gone-Wrong | Universal | Heist narrative whose pleasure is the failure of the plan — at some point the elaborate setup unravels, and the rest of the story is the consequences.
339 | Crime of Passion narrative | Universal | Crime committed in sudden emotional storm — jealous love, betrayed loyalty, witnessed cruelty. Unplanned, single-event character; the genre's question is moral attribution.
340 | High Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy set in a fully invented secondary world with its own geography, history, languages, and metaphysics. Total imaginative immersion.
341 | Sword and Sorcery | Universal | Fantasy focused on a wandering hero (often a barbarian or rogue) confronting magical antagonists in self-contained adventures. Smaller scale, looser world-building.
342 | Portal Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy in which protagonists from the everyday world cross into a magical world through a specific portal — wardrobe, rabbit-hole, mirror. The crossing itself is structural.
343 | Grimdark Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy that systematically subverts heroic conventions — protagonists morally compromised, victories pyrrhic, world brutal. Fantasy's complicity with violence brought into focus.
344 | Urban Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy set in modern urban environments where magic, supernatural creatures, or hidden worlds operate alongside ordinary life. Contemporary setting, noir-influenced register.
345 | Romantasy (Romance + Fantasy) | Universal | Hybrid combining fantasy world-building with central romantic plot. Romance gets equal or primary weight.
346 | Court Intrigue Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy centered on royal courts — succession, scheming, marriage politics, advisers' rivalries. Magic often present but secondary to political maneuvering.
347 | Dark Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy with horror's atmosphere and stakes — gothic darkness, body-horror creatures, ambiguous magic. Distinct from horror by its fantasy structure; from grimdark by supernatural-rather-than-cynical register.
348 | Fairy Tale Retelling | Universal | Modern reworking of classical fairy tale — often inverting, complicating, or contemporizing. Pleasure is recognition and revision of an inherited tale.
349 | Mythopoeia | Universal | Original myth-creation — fiction that invents new mythology rather than retelling existing ones. Mythic register and ambition.
350 | Dying Earth | Universal | Fantasy set in a world at the very end of its time — sun dimming, magic fading, civilizations collapsed into ruin. Melancholy register and end-of-the-world atmosphere as constant.
351 | Mythic Fiction | Universal | Modern fiction that explicitly draws on mythic structures and figures, often setting them in contemporary context. More reverent treatment of myth than urban fantasy.
352 | Fabulism | Universal | Short fiction in the mode of fable — talking animals, allegorical figures, magical events — but composed for adult audiences with modern sensibility.
353 | Progression Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy emphasizing the protagonist's continuous, measurable progression in skill, magical power, or rank — often related to Eastern xianxia/cultivation but in Western worlds.
354 | Chivalric Romance (Medieval) | Universal | Medieval European narrative form featuring noble knights on quests of honor, courtly love, and religious devotion. Specifically medieval-Christian context.
355 | Arthurian narrative | Universal | Vast cycle of narratives concerning King Arthur, the Knights of the Round Table, and the Matter of Britain. Distinct sub-cycle within chivalric romance with its own conventions.
356 | Folk-Tale (general European) | Universal | Pan-European folk-tale form: short, formulaic narrative with stock figures, magical objects, and didactic close. Oral-traditional register.
357 | Historical Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy set in a real historical period with magic added — Tudor England with witches, Imperial Rome with gods, Napoleonic Europe with dragons.
358 | Faerie / Fae narrative | Universal | Fantasy centered on the Fae — beautiful, dangerous, alien, bound by strange laws. Faithfulness to specific folkloric conventions (Celtic and Germanic).
359 | Sword and Planet | Universal | Hybrid SF/fantasy: Earthling protagonist transported to alien planet engages in sword-and-sorcery-style adventure. Distinct from sword-and-sorcery by extraterrestrial setting.
360 | Wainscot Society fantasy | Universal | Fantasy in which a hidden community lives within or behind the structures of mundane society — borrowers in walls, secret magic schools, hidden wizard governments. The masquerade is structural.
361 | Comic Fantasy | Universal | Fantasy whose primary register is humor — satirizing high fantasy conventions, playing with genre tropes, mixing absurdity into world-building. Laughter as structural payoff.
362 | Romantic Fantasy | Universal | Older variant of romantasy: fantasy adventure in which a romance arc is central but not necessarily commercially-engineered. Less formula-driven, often deeper world-building.
363 | Quiet Fantasy / Cozy Fantasy | Universal | Low-stakes, contemplative fantasy. World is magical but conflicts are small: opening a coffee shop with magical animals, growing potion herbs, mending the village.
364 | Saga Fantasy (multi-generational) | Universal | Fantasy that follows multiple generations of a family, dynasty, or line — each generation a major arc. Familial-temporal sweep.
365 | Body Horror | Universal | Horror grounded in the violation, transformation, or grotesque alteration of the body. Visceral, embodied focus.
366 | Slasher | Universal | Horror centered on a killer (often masked, often supernatural) systematically eliminating victims — usually young people. Highly conventionalized ritual unfolding of violence.
367 | Found Footage Horror | Universal | Horror presented as if assembled from real recordings discovered after the events — handheld camera, security tapes, smartphone video. Documentary-fictional structure.
368 | Folk Horror | Universal | Horror rooted in landscape, isolated communities, and ancient pagan or folk practice. Rural and pre-Christian register; terror from older orders the present has not erased.
369 | Possession narrative | Universal | Horror in which a person's body is invaded and controlled by a malevolent supernatural entity. Spiritual rather than physical horror; loss of self to outside will.
370 | Survival Horror | Universal | Horror grounded in protagonist's struggle to survive in environment systematically hostile — limited resources, lurking threats, attrition. Resource-management and endurance over confrontation.
371 | Psychological Horror | Universal | Horror grounded in mental disturbance — paranoia, dread, dissociation, unreliable perception. Interior register; threat may be internal or ambiguous.
372 | Vampire Narrative | Universal | Horror or fantasy centered on vampires — undead bloodsuckers with their own conventions. Aversion to sunlight and crosses, transformation through bite, eternal life, predatory romance.
373 | Werewolf / Lycanthropy Narrative | Universal | Horror centered on humans who transform into wolves or wolf-like creatures — through curse, bite, or family inheritance. Body-transformation horror and dual-nature theme.
374 | Zombie Narrative | Universal | Horror centered on the dead returning as mindless flesh-eaters. The zombie has become a dominant horror metaphor for various societal anxieties.
375 | Haunted House | Universal | Horror in which the threat is the house itself — its history, its spirits, its inability to let people leave. Setting-as-antagonist structure.
376 | Splatterpunk | Universal | Extreme horror distinguished by graphic violence, gore, and transgressive content. Visceral, body-explicit register.
377 | Quiet Horror | Universal | Horror that operates through suggestion, atmosphere, and what is NOT shown rather than gore or shock. Restraint and psychological depth.
378 | Cult Horror | Universal | Horror in which the threat is an organized group — religious cult, secret society, hidden community — pursuing collective dark purpose. Human-collective threat.
379 | Witch Narrative | Universal | Horror or fantasy centered on witches — old crones, hidden covens, accused women. Spans victim narratives (Salem-style) and supernatural-power narratives (Macbeth-style).
380 | Demonic Possession Horror | Universal | Horror sub-form: demonic-specific (vs ghost-possession) variants. Specifically Christian-demonological register with priest as exorcist.
381 | Eco-Horror | Universal | Horror in which the threat is nature itself, often as response to human ecological transgression — forests that fight back, animals that rebel, environments that consume.
382 | Gothic Romance (genre fusion) | Universal | Hybrid of gothic horror and romance — atmospheric mansion, dangerous Byronic love interest, family secret, bride in peril. Romance gets equal structural weight.
383 | Sci-Fi Horror | Universal | Horror in science-fiction settings — alien parasites, malevolent AI, body-snatcher infiltration. Rational-scientific grounding of the horror element.
384 | J-Horror (modern Japanese horror) | Universal | Modern Japanese horror genre centered on technological haunting, female ghosts, slow-build psychological dread. Distinct from classical kaidan by media-and-technology setting.
385 | Korean Horror (modern) | Universal | Modern Korean horror genre combining family/social horror, supernatural elements, and political subtext. Korean-specific cultural texture (Confucian family pressure, shamanic elements, han).
386 | Slow-Burn Horror | Universal | Horror that develops dread gradually across an extended runtime — minimal jump scares, accumulated unease, climactic payoff that earns the wait. Patience as structural feature.

390 | Apocalyptic Horror | Universal | Horror tied to civilizational end — the last days, final judgment, cosmic curtain. The apocalypse itself is the horror, not just its aftermath.
391 | Slasher (procedural/franchise) | Universal | Procedural slasher in which killer's mythology is the primary content (Freddy, Jason, Michael) and victim-cycle structure is iterated across sequels. Killer-as-franchise.
392 | Horror Comedy | Universal | Hybrid where horror's conventions are played for laughter without abandoning real horror beats. Genuine horror stakes alongside comedy.
393 | Contemporary Romance | Universal | Romance set in the present day with realistic settings — workplace, dating apps, modern friendship circles. Now-context.
394 | Historical Romance | Universal | Romance set in a specific historical period — period's social rules and constraints integral to the romantic obstacle. Careful period detail.

404 | Workplace Romance | Universal | Romance set in a shared workplace — colleague dynamics, professional ambition, the workplace's rules complicating the relationship.
405 | Star-Crossed Lovers | Universal | Romance whose external impossibility is woven into the universe itself — fate, family feud, war, religious schism. Cosmic-fatalistic register.
