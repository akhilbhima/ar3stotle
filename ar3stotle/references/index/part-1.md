# Structure index, part 1 of 4 (ids 1-250)

One per line: `id | name | tradition | what it carries`. Read for emotional register, not format keywords.

1 | Kishōtenketsu narrative (起承転結) | Japanese | The canonical 4-beat East Asian narrative form. Use when the story should land through recognition rather than resolution.
2 | Noh play (能 / 五段) | Japanese | Classical Japanese masked theater in 5 dan following the jo-ha-kyū rhythmic principle. Use when the story requires sustained meditative pace before a concentrated payoff — appropriate for ghost-story, dream-narrative, and mystic-suggestion forms.
3 | Sewamono / Domestic tragedy (世話物) | Japanese | Edo-period bunraku/kabuki domestic tragedy featuring merchant-class lovers caught between giri (duty) and ninjō (feeling), often resolved through love-suicide. Use when the protagonist must be destroyed by impossibility of choosing between duty and feeling.
4 | Rakugo (落語) | Japanese | Classical Japanese comic monologue. Use when the story is dialogue-heavy and turns on a final wordplay or twist.
5 | Haibun travel narrative (俳文紀行) | Japanese | Hybrid prose-and-haiku travel form where each location's experience crystallizes in a haiku. Use when the narrative is journey-shaped and emotionally compressed.
6 | Kaidan / Japanese ghost story (怪談) | Japanese | Japanese supernatural tale whose horror operates through quiet intrusion. The uncanny enters everyday life calmly and is rarely defeated, instead absorbed with the residue of impermanence.
7 | Iyashikei / Healing slice-of-life (癒し系) | Japanese | Gentle low-stakes narrative form in which small disturbances are absorbed back into a slightly-evolved equilibrium. Use when the story should soothe rather than excite.
8 | Mono no aware narrative (物の哀れ) | Japanese | A narrative whose entire arc bends toward the recognition that something beautiful is fleeting — the bittersweet awareness of impermanence. Use when the payoff is recognition without resolution.
9 | Wabi-sabi narrative (侘寂) | Japanese | Narrative whose central reframe is the reinterpretation of imperfection as essential rather than broken. Use when the story should invert the reader's relationship to flawed things.
10 | Mu-ending narrative (無の物語) | Japanese | Narrative that ends in deliberate emptiness, silence, or absence. The void is not interpretation-pending — it IS the resolved state.
11 | Yūgen narrative (幽玄) | Japanese | Narrative whose payoff is an intimation — never a statement — of something profound and unsayable beneath the surface. The unsaid is the point.
12 | Watakushi-shōsetsu / I-novel (私小説) | Japanese | Distinctly Japanese confessional autobiographical fiction. The narrator-protagonist is barely fictionalized; the moral register is unflinching self-exposure without redemptive shape.
13 | Gi-seung-jeon-gyeol narrative (기승전결) | Korean | Korean reading of the East Asian 4-beat form. Same structure as kishōtenketsu, used distinctly in Korean essays, sijo poetry, and modern literary fiction.
14 | Sijo poetry-narrative (시조) | Korean | 3-line classical Korean poetic form (~43-45 syllables) compressing an entire narrative arc into three movements. The third line characteristically introduces a sharp turn.
15 | Pansori narrative (판소리) | Korean | Classical Korean epic-song form. A single sorikkun carries the narrative through alternating spoken (aniri) and sung (sori) passages, with audience cries (chuimsae) as structural punctuation.
16 | Han narrative (한 서사) | Korean | Narrative whose engine is han — accumulated, inherited, or unspoken sorrow. The payoff is a release that acknowledges rather than erases the wound.
17 | Jeong narrative (정 서사) | Korean | Narrative whose payoff is recognition of a deep, unspoken affectionate bond that was always there. Jeong is built through accumulation, not declaration.
18 | Sageuk / Korean historical court drama (사극) | Korean | Korean historical narrative set in the Joseon court. Indirection is structural — every action must occur within yangban propriety.
19 | Hyo / Filial-piety narrative (효 서사) | Korean | Narrative built around a child's extreme sacrifice for a parent. The genre's force is in the completeness of acceptance, not internal struggle.
20 | Heungbu-style folk tale (흥부형 민담) | Korean | Two-sibling Korean folk tale where moral character is revealed through how each treats the weak. The agent of justice is nature, not human authority.
21 | Sin-myeong shamanic narrative (신명 서사 / 굿) | Korean | Narrative culminating in collective ecstatic release — kut shamanic ritual, mask-dance climax. Resolution is experiential and ecstatic rather than logical.
22 | Qǐ-chéng-zhuǎn-hé narrative (起承轉合) | Chinese | Original Tang-dynasty 4-beat structure that gave rise to kishōtenketsu and gi-seung-jeon-gyeol. Foundational to classical Chinese poetry, especially jueju.
23 | Wuxia / Martial-hero narrative (武俠) | Chinese | Narrative of a martial-arts protagonist navigating the jianghu — the underworld of rival sects, sworn brotherhoods, and unwritten honor codes. Defining tension is conflict between feeling, code, and political duty.
24 | Xianxia / Cultivation-fantasy narrative (仙俠) | Chinese | Narrative of a cultivator's ascent through measurable tiers of spiritual/martial advancement. The structural payoff is advancement itself; each breakthrough is a beat.
25 | Sanguo-style strategic narrative (三國演義式) | Chinese | Historical-strategic narrative whose payoff is the disclosure of long-laid plans. The strategist's perspective is foregrounded, not the spy/detective's discovery.
26 | Daoist sage parable (道家寓言) | Chinese | Short philosophical narrative whose payoff is the Dào revelation — non-conceptual insight via paradox, animal-fable, or skilled artisan. Resolves through wuwei.
27 | Confucian moral exemplum (儒家典範故事) | Chinese | Short didactic narrative built to demonstrate a Confucian virtue through a single exemplary action. Force comes from the completeness of acceptance.
28 | Chuanqi / Tang marvelous tale (傳奇) | Chinese | Short literary tale mixing strange (foxes, ghosts, immortals) with the human (love, examinations, journeys). The supernatural is moral mirror.
29 | Zhiguai / Anomaly record (志怪) | Chinese | Short tale recording a strange event in documentary register, as if reporting fact. Distinct from chuanqi by being shorter and more cataloging.
30 | Caizi-jiaren / Scholar-and-beauty romance (才子佳人) | Chinese | Ming-Qing romance pairing scholar with talented young woman, overcoming social obstacles toward happy union. Lovers court through composed verse.
31 | Shenmo / Gods-and-demons fantasy (神魔) | Chinese | Long-form fantasy where a mortal protagonist traverses a cosmology of gods, demons, immortals. Signature: cosmic, picaresque, episodic scale.
32 | Pinghua / Oral storytelling chapter-novel (評話 / 說書) | Chinese | Long narrative composed for oral performance, structured around chapter-end cliffhangers. Audience-aware pacing built into the form.
33 | Pañca-Sandhi drama (पञ्चसन्धि) | Indian | 5-juncture structure of Sanskrit drama from Bharata's Nāṭyaśāstra. Use when the story should have classical formal symmetry with a deliberate pause before final movement.
34 | Navarasa-driven narrative (नवरस) | Indian | Narrative organized to evoke the nine rasas systematically — śṛṅgāra, hāsya, karuṇā, raudra, vīra, bhayānaka, bībhatsa, adbhuta, śānta. Use when the structural ambition is emotional.
35 | Itihasa epic / Ramayana 7-kāṇḍa (इतिहास) | Indian | Classical Indian historical-mythical epic. Use when narrative requires civilizational scope.
36 | Jataka tale (जातक) | Indian | Buddhist birth-story: a present situation explained by a past life of the Bodhisattva, illustrating karmic root of present circumstance. 547 canonical Jatakas in Pali.
37 | Pañchatantra fable (पञ्चतन्त्र) | Indian | Nested-frame collection of moral animal fables organized into five books, each demonstrating a principle of practical wisdom (nīti).
38 | Kāvya / Refined poetic narrative (काव्य) | Indian | Classical Sanskrit refined literary narrative — ornate description, elaborate similes, attention to figures of sound and sense. Mahākāvya canonical structure.
39 | Champu / Mixed prose-verse (चम्पू) | Indian | Classical Indian narrative form combining prose and verse passages — prose for action, verse for emotional intensification. Oscillates between fast prose movement and lyrical lingering.
40 | Sangam Akam / Interior love-narrative (அகம்) | Indian | Classical Tamil love-poetry-narrative classified by tiṇai (landscape-mood). Mountain (kuṟiñci) for union, forest (mullai) for separation, etc.
41 | Sangam Puram / Exterior heroic narrative (புறம்) | Indian | Classical Tamil exterior-life narrative: war, kings, gifts, public death, the ethics of the warrior. Counterpart to akam.
42 | Bhakti devotional narrative (भक्ति) | Indian | Devotional poem-narrative whose engine is the lover-devotee's longing for the divine beloved. The relationship between human and divine is structured as romance.
43 | Maqama / Rhymed-prose picaresque (مقامة) | Persian-Arabic | Short rhymed-prose tale featuring a recurring eloquent rogue who deceives through dazzling display of language. Episodic; collections of 50-100 maqamat per author.
44 | Dastan / Oral epic (داستان) | Persian-Arabic | Long oral epic narrative shared across Persian, Arabic, Turkic, Urdu, and South Asian Muslim cultures. Performed by professional dastango.
45 | Mathnawi / Sufi narrative poem (مثنوی) | Persian-Arabic | Long narrative poem in rhyming couplets, often loosely-structured as chain of stories illustrating Sufi teaching. Stories teach by accumulation rather than direct moral.
46 | Frame-tale / Thousand and One Nights (ألف ليلة وليلة) | Persian-Arabic | Frame-narrative form where outer story (Scheherazade) contains hundreds of nested tales. The frame's tension (death deferred by storytelling) saturates every sub-tale.
47 | Sufi parable (حكمة) | Persian-Arabic | Short illustrative tale delivering Sufi spiritual point through paradox, reversal, or apparent foolishness — often featuring a wise fool. Surface absurdity is the door to deeper truth.
48 | Marsiya / Karbala elegy (مرثیة) | Persian-Arabic | Shi'a Islamic elegiac narrative retelling Husayn's martyrdom at Karbala, performed during Muharram. Communal sacred grief; collective re-living of originating martyrdom.
49 | Sirah / Prophetic biography (سيرة) | Persian-Arabic | Biographical narrative of the Prophet Muhammad assembled from hadith and chains of transmission. Citation-chains ground every event.
50 | Adab essay-narrative (أدب) | Persian-Arabic | Refined essayistic prose tradition combining ethical instruction, historical anecdote, witty dialogue, poetry, and personal observation. Cultivated worldliness.
51 | Griot epic / Sundiata model | West-African | Long oral epic performed by hereditary jeli/griot, recounting the founding figure of an empire or lineage. Integrates praise-song, history, genealogy, proverbs, kora/balafon.
52 | Anansi trickster cycle | West-African | Akan trickster tales featuring Anansi the spider outwitting stronger animals through cunning. Carried to Caribbean and Americas via slave trade.
53 | Ifá ese / Divination story | West-African | Yoruba divination narrative attached to one of the 256 odu. When a babalawo casts and an odu emerges, its corresponding ese is recited.
54 | Yoruba oriki praise-narrative (Oríkì) | West-African | Yoruba praise-narrative composed of densely-packed honorifics, historical references, and metaphoric epithets. Identity-establishing rather than progressive.
55 | Dilemma tale | West-African | Short folk tale ending in an open question rather than resolution. The community discussion IS the form's completion.
56 | Pourquoi tale / Why-story | West-African | Short folk tale explaining why some natural feature is the way it is. Common across West African traditions and globally.
57 | Funeral praise-oration / Akan funeral | West-African | Funeral praise-narrative integrating biography, lineage praise, ancestral invocation. The deceased is being escorted into the community of ancestors.
58 | Call-and-response narrative | West-African | Communal narrative form where leader recites and audience responds. Co-produced; foundational to spirituals, gospel, blues, hip-hop.
59 | Diné Hózhǫ́jí / Beauty Way ceremony-narrative | Indigenous-Americas | Ceremonial narrative structured around restoration of hózhǫ́ (balance, beauty, harmony) through four directional movements. The story returns disrupted person, family, or world to balance.
60 | Coyote trickster cycle | Indigenous-Americas | Widely-distributed trickster cycle across Plains, Southwest, Plateau Indigenous traditions. Coyote as both creator and fool.
61 | Popol Vuh creation cycle | Indigenous-Americas | K'iche' Maya creation narrative — origin of world, multiple creation-failures, Hero Twins' descent into Xibalba, emergence of present world.
62 | Lakota star-knowledge cycle | Indigenous-Americas | Lakota narrative cycle in which sacred stories are mapped onto stars and constellations, with corresponding earthly events following seasonal star movements.
63 | Iroquois Great Law origin narrative (Kayanerenkó:wa) | Indigenous-Americas | Haudenosaunee origin narrative of the Great Law of Peace — founding of the Confederacy by the Peacemaker and Hiawatha. Collective political form emerges from individual mourning.
64 | Inuit Sedna narrative | Indigenous-Americas | Inuit narrative cycle of Sedna (Nuliajuk) — sea-mother whose body parts become marine animals. Encodes ethical relationships with hunted animals.
65 | Nanabozho trickster-creator cycle | Indigenous-Americas | Anishinaabe narrative cycle of Nanabozho — culture-hero, trickster, creator. World-shaping is byproduct of foolish appetite rather than divine intention.
66 | Haudenosaunee Thanksgiving Address (Ohén:ton Karihwatéhkwen) | Indigenous-Americas | Haudenosaunee opening prayer-narrative recited before all gatherings. Each beat: "We give thanks to [X]; now our minds are one." Gratitude as cosmology.
67 | Icelandic family saga (Íslendingasögur) | Celtic-Norse | Icelandic family saga: prose narrative of multi-generational feuds in Iceland's settlement period, composed in 13th-14th c. with restrained, almost documentary register.
68 | Echtrae / Irish otherworld adventure | Celtic-Norse | Early Irish narrative form — hero is invited to the Otherworld, returns to find mortal world transformed or aged. Dangerous porousness between worlds.
69 | Immram / Irish sea-voyage | Celtic-Norse | Early Irish sea-voyage narrative through sequence of strange islands, each with its own marvel or ordeal. Episodic-allegorical journey.
70 | Táin / Cattle-raid epic (Táin Bó Cúailnge) | Celtic-Norse | Central narrative of the Irish Ulster Cycle. Queen Medb raids Ulster for the great brown bull; Cú Chulainn alone defends through single combats.
71 | Mabinogi / Welsh Four Branches (Pedair Cainc y Mabinogi) | Celtic-Norse | Four Branches of the Welsh Mabinogi — structurally interlinked sequence of medieval Welsh prose narratives following different but connected protagonists.
72 | Eddic mythological poem (Eddukvæði) | Celtic-Norse | Norse mythological poetry in alliterative meter — cosmogony, wisdom-dialogue, heroic-legendary, god-narratives. Mythologically dense rather than character-interior.
73 | Aisling / Irish vision-poem | Celtic-Norse | Irish form: a poet falls asleep and is visited by a spéirbhean ("sky-woman") personifying Ireland. Highly conventionalized political-allegorical bardic form.
74 | Þáttr / Norse short tale | Celtic-Norse | Short Norse tale, often embedded in king's-saga, where Icelander visits Norwegian court, is initially dismissed, then proves himself.
75 | Magical realism (Realismo mágico) | Latin-American | Narrative mode where fantastic events occur within otherwise realistic settings and are received by characters as ordinary. The supernatural is woven into daily life's texture.
76 | Lo Real Maravilloso / The marvelous real | Latin-American | Carpentier's term: where magical realism inserts the marvelous into the real, lo real maravilloso asserts that Latin American reality itself is already marvelous.
77 | Testimonio | Latin-American | First-person witness narrative, often spoken by a marginalized person and edited by another. Bearing witness on behalf of a community.
78 | Crónica / Latin American chronicle | Latin-American | Literary-journalistic narrative form blending essay, reportage, and short story. The narrator's voice is essayistic; events are real but rendered with literary attention.
79 | Boom novel / Multi-perspective complexity | Latin-American | Latin American Boom novelistic form — structurally complex, multi-perspective, time-fragmented, politically engaged. Formal experimentation mirrors political-historical complexity.
80 | Gauchesque narrative (Literatura gauchesca) | Latin-American | Argentine-Uruguayan narrative featuring the gaucho — pampas horseman — as protagonist. Frontier-pastoral with state-conflict undertone.
81 | Indigenismo narrative | Latin-American | Narrative form (primarily Andean and Mesoamerican) that places Indigenous experience and worldview at the center, critiquing the criollo state's ongoing dispossession.
82 | Costumbrismo / Manners narrative | Latin-American | 19th-c. literary form focused on close observation of regional customs, types, manners, folk life.
83 | Aristotelian three-act structure | Universal | Foundational Western dramatic structure. Every action has a beginning, middle, and end.
84 | Freytag's Pyramid / 5-act structure | Universal | Gustav Freytag's 5-act model derived from Greek and Shakespearean tragedy. The pyramid reflects rise toward climax and fall after.
85 | Overcoming the Monster (Booker) | Universal | Booker's plot 1: protagonist learns of great evil, prepares, faces apparent defeat, then defeats the monster. Use when threat is singular, embodied, must be confronted directly.
86 | Rags to Riches (Booker) | Universal | Booker's plot 2: poor figure rises to wealth, falls back into adversity, then rises permanently with mature self-knowledge. Deserved elevation tested by reversal.
87 | The Quest (Booker) | Universal | Booker's plot 3: hero learns of precious object/place, journeys with companions, faces ordeals, obtains the goal. External-goal-oriented.
88 | Voyage and Return (Booker) | Universal | Booker's plot 4: protagonist travels into strange world, has wondrous and threatening experiences, escapes home transformed. No specific goal — the journey IS the structure.
89 | Comedy (Booker) | Universal | Booker's plot 5: society in confusion deepens to apparent impasse, resolved with everyone reconciled. Confusion resolved into community.
90 | Tragedy (Booker) | Universal | Booker's plot 6: protagonist commits to a course driven by ego-attachment that gradually consumes them, leading inevitably to their destruction.
91 | Rebirth (Booker) | Universal | Booker's plot 7: protagonist falls under dark power, is gradually swallowed, then redeemed by love or sacrifice that breaks the spell.
92 | Quest (Tobias) | Universal | Tobias's character-focused quest: external goal is placeholder for self-discovery. Use when external goal is secondary to internal transformation.
93 | Adventure (Tobias) | Universal | Tobias's action-focused adventure: series of incidents drives protagonist forward. Action, novelty, travel for their own sake.
94 | Pursuit (Tobias) | Universal | One character pursues another. The chase IS the plot.
95 | Rescue (Tobias) | Universal | Hero must save a victim from a villain. Three-cornered structure: hero, villain, victim.
96 | Escape (Tobias) | Universal | Protagonist is trapped and must escape. The protagonist saves themselves.
97 | Revenge (Tobias) | Universal | Protagonist is wronged and seeks retribution. The genre's question is whether revenge will satisfy or destroy.
98 | The Riddle (Tobias) | Universal | Puzzle at the heart of the narrative; reader and protagonist work together to solve it. The pleasure is the deduction.
99 | Rivalry (Tobias) | Universal | Two protagonists locked in opposition, each defined by the other. Symmetrical — both characters are full agents.
100 | Underdog (Tobias) | Universal | Sustained narrative of unlikely hero rising against more powerful opposition. Disadvantage as the fuel for an extended arc.
101 | Temptation (Tobias) | Universal | Protagonist faces moral test in the form of attractive but forbidden choice. Internal struggle is the center.
102 | Metamorphosis (Tobias) | Universal | Character undergoes literal physical or supernatural transformation. Distinct from internal Transformation: this is external/visible.
103 | Transformation (Tobias) | Universal | Internal transformation arc. Character is fundamentally changed by experience.
104 | Maturation (Tobias) | Universal | Coming-of-age plot. Young protagonist confronts adult world and matures through hardship.
105 | Love (Tobias) | Universal | Two people meet, kept apart by obstacle, come together. Classical love-plot in simplest form.
106 | Forbidden Love (Tobias) | Universal | Love is forbidden by society, family, or moral law — and that prohibition IS the plot. Love against social structure.
107 | Sacrifice (Tobias) | Universal | Protagonist must give up something dear to achieve a greater good. Climax is the sacrifice itself.
108 | Discovery (Tobias) | Universal | Character uncovers truth about themselves — identity, origins, or past. Investigation is biographical-internal.
109 | Wretched Excess (Tobias) | Universal | Protagonist destroyed by their own appetites — gluttony, lust, ambition, addiction. Excess of a single drive consuming its host.
110 | Ascension (Tobias) | Universal | Character rises to greatness — moral, social, or accomplishment-based. Upward-arc; climax is achievement of stature.
111 | Descension (Tobias) | Universal | Character falls from great position. Fall-arc; gradual loss of stature, position, or self.
112 | Polti's 36 Dramatic Situations (taxonomic) | Universal | Polti's exhaustive catalog of every dramatic situation. Use as scene-design vocabulary.
113 | Campbell's Monomyth / Hero's Journey | Universal | Joseph Campbell's seventeen-stage cyclical structure from cross-cultural mythological analysis. Departure, Initiation, Return.
114 | Vogler's Writer's Journey (12-stage) | Universal | Christopher Vogler's screenwriting-friendly distillation of Campbell's Monomyth into 12 stages, designed for Hollywood film structure.
115 | Harmon's Story Circle (8-stage) | Universal | Dan Harmon's compressed 8-step Hero's Journey, used in TV writing (Community, Rick and Morty). Emphasizes return-to-changed-self.
116 | Save the Cat (15-beat) | Universal | Blake Snyder's 15-beat screenplay structure, with each beat assigned to a specific page range in a 110-page screenplay. Highly prescriptive Hollywood feature standard.
117 | Truby's 22-Step Anatomy | Universal | John Truby's 22-step structure: built around moral premise, ghost (past wound), desire, opponent, plan, battle, self-revelation. Story as organic moral argument.
118 | Propp's 31 Folktale Functions | Universal | Vladimir Propp's structural analysis of 100 Russian folk tales yielded 31 narrative functions in fixed order. Foundational to structuralist narratology.
119 | In medias res / Homeric epic structure | Universal | Classical epic structural convention: narrative begins in middle of action (typically near climax), uses flashback or framed retelling for earlier events.
120 | Murdock's Heroine's Journey | Universal | Maureen Murdock's 10-stage cyclical journey: separation from feminine, identification with masculine, eventual disillusionment, reintegration with wounded feminine self.
121 | Hudson's Virgin's Promise | Universal | Kim Hudson's 13-stage feminine creative-emergence journey. Protagonist begins in suppressive community, secretly cultivates her gift, is exposed, ultimately transforms her community.
122 | Vonnegut's Six Story Shapes (taxonomic) | Universal | Kurt Vonnegut's emotional-arc taxonomy. Stories plotted on good fortune/ill fortune over time yield one of six recurring shapes.
123 | Expository documentary | Universal | Voice-of-God narration directly addresses viewer to convey information, building an argument supported by visual evidence. Most traditional documentary mode.
124 | Observational documentary | Universal | Fly-on-the-wall mode. Camera observes events without narration, interviews, or apparent intervention.
125 | Participatory documentary | Universal | Filmmaker is visibly present, interacting with subjects. The documentary's truth comes from the encounter between filmmaker and subject.
126 | Reflexive documentary | Universal | Documentary that draws attention to its own constructedness — to the conventions, the politics of representation, the impossibility of pure objectivity.
127 | Performative documentary | Universal | Foregrounds filmmaker's subjective experience and emotional truth — personal essay, autobiography, embodied perspective. Rejects objectivity in favor of "what this means to me."
128 | Poetic documentary | Universal | Privileges mood, rhythm, visual association over linear argument. Often non-narrative, organized by visual/aural patterns.
129 | Monroe's Motivated Sequence | Universal | Alan Monroe's 5-step persuasive speech structure (1935). Specifically engineered for behavior change — not just informing but motivating action.
130 | Toulmin Argument Model | Universal | Stephen Toulmin's 6-component model of practical argument. Distinguishes from formal logic by acknowledging probability and qualification.
131 | Rogerian Argument | Universal | Carl Rogers-influenced argumentative form built around establishing common ground before advancing one's position. Use when audience holds opposing views and adversarial rhetoric would harden positions.
132 | Socratic Dialogue | Universal | Plato's didactic dialogue form. Teacher leads interlocutor through questions that expose confused premises.
133 | Aristotelian Triple Appeal (Ethos/Pathos/Logos) | Universal | Aristotle's three modes of persuasion. Not a sequential beat structure but a triad of registers any persuasive text combines.
134 | Inverted Pyramid (journalism) | Universal | Defining structure of news writing — most important information first, supporting details next, background last. Designed for telegraph-era news.
135 | Nut-graf feature lead | Universal | Feature-style structure: anecdotal opening followed by paragraph explaining why the story matters. The reader is invited in by scene before being told the stakes.
136 | Tick-Tock | Universal | Reconstructed-chronology narrative. A major event retold beat-by-beat in real time, often using interviews and documents.
137 | Profile / Portrait | Universal | Journalistic biographical narrative of a single person. Present scene → backstory → present significance.
138 | Long-form literary journalism | Universal | Extended reported nonfiction using techniques of fiction — scene-by-scene, full dialogue, interior perspective, status detail — to render real events.
139 | Investigative narrative | Universal | Reported narrative whose engine is the journalist's investigation itself — gradual uncovering of hidden facts, often involving institutional wrongdoing.
140 | Case Study | Universal | Structured narrative of specific business situation, decision, and outcome. Used in education, consulting, B2B marketing.
141 | Pitch Deck Story | Universal | Narrative arc of a startup investor pitch. Engineered for 10-15 slides and 15 minutes.
142 | Brand Origin Story | Universal | Company's foundational narrative — founder's moment of insight, unmet need perceived, early struggle, present significance. Creates emotional attachment to brand.
143 | Customer Success Story | Universal | Narrative of specific customer's transformation through using a product. Before-state → catalyst → after-state, with measurable outcomes.
144 | Crisis Communication | Universal | Structured corporate response to scandal, accident, or public failure. Acknowledge harm, accept responsibility, articulate corrective action, restore trust.
145 | Disruption Narrative | Universal | Christensen's framing as public narrative: incumbent industry leader overtaken by smaller competitor offering "worse" product that redefines market.
146 | White Paper | Universal | Long-form authoritative document defining a problem, examining existing solutions, advocating a specific approach. Used in B2B marketing, policy advocacy.
147 | IMRAD Research Paper | Universal | Standard structure of empirical scientific papers. Designed for reproducibility and rapid expert reading.
148 | Literature Review | Universal | Academic synthesis of existing research — a structured map of the field. Identifies major findings, contested questions, methodological approaches, gaps.
149 | Position Paper / Op-Ed | Universal | Short-to-medium argumentative essay advocating a specific position. Compressed for journalistic or institutional reading.
150 | Grant Proposal | Universal | Structured request for funding research, programs, or projects. Distinguished by dual audience: program officers and reviewers.
151 | Opening Statement (legal) | Universal | First address by lawyer to jury, previewing the case. Strictly bounded — must be roadmap, not argument; promises evidence rather than asserting conclusions.
152 | Closing Argument (legal) | Universal | Lawyer's final address to jury, connecting evidence to verdict requested. Argument is permitted; lawyer is now persuading rather than previewing.
153 | Stump Speech | Universal | Repeated political speech delivered across many campaign stops. Modular and emotionally consistent — same emotional arc through varying details.
154 | Inaugural Address | Universal | Ceremonial speech by incoming head of state. Distinguished by move from contested partisanship to symbolic unity.
155 | Concession Speech | Universal | Speech of defeated candidate acknowledging loss and legitimacy of opponent's victory. The transfer of legitimacy IS the engine.
156 | Manifesto | Universal | Declarative public statement of intent or principles by a movement. Designed to provoke alignment and rupture.
157 | Whistleblower Narrative | Universal | First-person account of insider exposing institutional wrongdoing, often at personal cost. Authority comes from inside position.
158 | Cosmic Horror (Lovecraftian) | Universal | Horror based on smallness of human consciousness against indifferent or malevolent cosmos. The threat is ontological, not personal.
159 | Gothic Horror | Universal | Horror anchored in atmospheric place — haunted house, ruined castle, decaying estate — and family secrets, suppressed sexuality, return of the buried past.
160 | Whodunit | Universal | Puzzle-mystery in which detective gathers clues to identify killer from closed cast of suspects. The pleasure is the deduction; clues are fairly available to careful reader.
161 | Hardboiled Detective | Universal | Detective story rooted in urban realism, moral ambiguity, cynical first-person narrator. The puzzle is secondary to atmosphere and detective's character.
162 | Cozy Mystery | Universal | Puzzle-mystery in low-violence pastoral or small-town setting with amateur detective. The world is one the reader wants to return to.
163 | Bildungsroman | Universal | European novel of formation. Young protagonist's psychological and moral development from naïve youth through formative trials to mature adulthood.
164 | Picaresque | Universal | Satirical episodic novel following low-born rogue through adventures across various social strata, exposing each through the rogue's wits. Pícaro does NOT mature — they survive.
165 | Refusal-of-the-Easy | Universal (NGC-original) | Short narrative whose entire arc is the protagonist's deliberate refusal of an obviously beneficial choice — not because they fail to see its value, but because accepting would betray something they refuse to name. 167 | Witness-Without-Intervention | Universal (NGC-original) | Narrative in which the protagonist is positioned as witness to something significant — usually injustice or beauty — and the structural payoff is the quality of the witnessing itself, not action taken.

169 | Ledger-Closes | Universal (NGC-original) | Narrative whose engine is settling a long-standing moral debt — not revenge (hot, proportional), but cool deliberate return of what was owed, after many years.
170 | The Held Question | Universal (NGC-original) | Narrative whose payoff is the deepening of a question rather than its resolution. The question itself ripens.
171 | Chosen One narrative | Universal | Protagonist singled out by prophecy, birth, or supernatural sign as uniquely fated. Distinct from Hero's Journey by its external selection — the call is not refuseable in any meaningful way.
172 | Fish Out of Water | Universal | Protagonist placed in environment radically alien to their habits and competencies; the friction between protagonist and setting drives both comedy and growth.
173 | Mentor and Pupil | Universal | Narrative centered on relationship between experienced master and inexperienced student. The mentor's wisdom is tested as much as the pupil's capacity.
174 | Buddy narrative | Universal | Two protagonists with contrasting temperaments, methods, or backgrounds paired (often forced together). Pleasure is the friction-and-bond between mismatched partners.
175 | Ensemble narrative | Universal | Narrative organized around a group of roughly equal-weight characters whose intersecting arcs constitute the story. The cast IS the story.
176 | Stranger Comes to Town | Universal | One of John Gardner's two foundational stories. Outsider's arrival disrupts static order of a community, exposing tensions and forcing reconfiguration.
177 | Hero Goes on a Journey | Universal | Gardner's other foundational story. Protagonist leaves the familiar and travels into the unknown.
178 | Sacrificial Lamb | Universal | Innocent character must die or suffer to redeem or save a community, often without their full understanding of why. The lamb may not even understand the role.
179 | Riches to Rags | Universal | Mirror of Booker's Rags-to-Riches. Character begins in wealth, status, or comfort and falls — through error, betrayal, or systemic collapse — into deprivation.
180 | Round Robin / Interlocking narrative | Universal | Narrative composed of multiple discrete stories that connect through shared characters, locations, themes, or coincidences. The reader's pleasure is recognition of connections.
181 | Narration mode | Universal | Telling a story — sequential events arranged in time. Use when the goal is to recount what happened.
182 | Description mode | Universal | Detailed sensory rendering of a person, place, object, or scene. Use when content is what something is like rather than what happens.
183 | Exposition mode | Universal | Explanation of an idea, concept, or process. Use when content is informational and the reader needs to grasp how something works.
184 | Argumentation mode | Universal | Reasoning to convince. Use when the goal is to advance a contested claim with evidence and reasoning.
185 | Comparison and Contrast | Universal | Structure organized around the systematic juxtaposition of two or more subjects. Use when understanding comes through what is like and what is unlike.
186 | Cause and Effect | Universal | Structure tracing causal chains — what produces what, and why. Use when content is why something happened or what something will produce.
187 | Process Analysis | Universal | Step-by-step explanation of how something is done or how something happens. Use when content is procedural.
188 | Definition mode | Universal | Establishing precisely what a term, concept, or category means — by genus, by negation, by example, by etymology. Use when content's success depends on a term being shared.
189 | Classification and Division | Universal | Organizing a complex domain into categories (classification) or breaking a single thing into its parts (division). Use when content is taxonomic.
190 | Syllogism | Universal | Aristotle's three-part deductive argument. The foundational form of formal reasoning.
191 | Enthymeme | Universal | Syllogism with one premise unstated — assumed shared with the audience. The most common form of real-world persuasive reasoning.
192 | Argument by Analogy | Universal | Reasoning that two cases share enough relevant features that what is true of one is likely true of the other. Use when direct evidence is absent but a comparable case can be invoked.
193 | Confirmation-Refutation | Universal | Classical rhetorical structure: first establish your own arguments positively, then dismantle the opposing view. Use when audience is mixed and both sides need addressing.
194 | Concession-and-Counter | Universal | A move within argument: explicitly grant a point to the opposing view, then redirect to a stronger point that holds despite the concession. Honest acknowledgment of partial agreement strengthens credibility.
195 | Reductio ad Absurdum | Universal | Showing that an opponent's premise leads to absurd or contradictory consequences, thereby disproving the premise.
196 | Bandwagon | Universal | Persuasion arguing "everyone is doing it / believes it / supports it" — therefore you should join. Exploits social proof.
197 | Glittering Generality | Universal | Use of vague, virtuous-sounding terms (freedom, justice, family values) without specific content — letting the audience fill in their own meaning.
198 | Card Stacking | Universal | Selective presentation of evidence — only the facts that support the position, omitting contradictory evidence.
199 | Plain Folks appeal | Universal | Speaker positions themselves as ordinary, just-like-you, to bypass elite-skepticism in the audience.
200 | Testimonial appeal | Universal | A respected or admired figure endorses the position or product. Audience trust in a third party transfers to the proposition.
201 | Transfer | Universal | Associating the proposition with a symbol, image, or institution that the audience already values (flag, religion, family) — transferring positive feeling.
202 | Name Calling | Universal | Discrediting the opposing position by attaching pejorative labels — without engaging the substance. Recognized propaganda technique; ethically loaded.
203 | Fear Appeal | Universal | Persuasion grounded in arousing fear of consequences if audience does not adopt the position. Powerful and ethically heavy.
204 | Editorial (institutional voice) | Universal | Unsigned argumentative piece representing the position of a publication's editorial board. Distinguished from op-eds by institutional weight.
205 | Letter to the Editor | Universal | Short, signed reader response to previous coverage. 200-400 words.
206 | Obituary | Universal | Biographical narrative of a recently-deceased person. Valedictory mode — what was this life for.
207 | Sports Feature | Universal | Long-form sports narrative going beyond game results to character, context, and meaning. The sports event or athlete carries broader human or cultural weight.
208 | Service Journalism / How-to | Universal | Practical instructional content presented in journalistic form. Blends process analysis with editorial selection.
209 | News Analysis | Universal | Signed journalist's interpretation of news events — distinguishing implications, providing context, identifying stakes. Distinct from news (reports) and op-ed (argues): analysis explains and frames.
210 | Listicle | Universal | Information presented as a numbered list with brief descriptions. Scannable structure: each item is self-contained.
211 | Annual Letter to Shareholders | Universal | CEO's signed reflection on the year's performance, strategy, and vision. Hybrid: financial accountability + brand storytelling + strategic communication.
212 | Quarterly Earnings Call narrative | Universal | Structured corporate narrative delivered to analysts and investors quarterly. Engineered for credibility under scrutiny.
213 | SWOT-Style Strategic Narrative | Universal | Strategic analysis organized as Strengths / Weaknesses / Opportunities / Threats — internal and external, positive and negative.
214 | OKR Story | Universal | Strategic narrative organized around Objectives (qualitative goals) and Key Results (quantitative measures). Used at Intel, Google, and across modern tech.
215 | Post-Mortem / Retrospective | Universal | Structured analysis of a project, incident, or failure after the fact, with the explicit goal of organizational learning rather than blame.
216 | Conference Paper / Talk | Universal | Short academic presentation (typically 15-20 minutes) at a conference. Compressed argument oriented toward in-room peer scrutiny.
217 | Dissertation Defense | Universal | Doctoral candidate's oral defense of their dissertation before a committee. High-stakes mastery demonstration under expert challenge.
218 | Academic Book Review | Universal | Scholarly review of an academic book, situating it within a field and assessing contributions. Emphasizes disciplinary conversation.
219 | Creation myth (cosmogony) | Universal | Narrative explaining how the world, universe, or fundamental order came to be. Establishes the most basic terms of reality.
220 | Flood myth | Universal | Widely-distributed narrative type. Divine displeasure leads to a great flood; chosen few survive to rebuild.
221 | Theogony | Universal | Narrative of the origin and genealogy of the gods. Distinguished from cosmogony by its specific concern with how the divine pantheon came into being and stratified.
222 | Hero-Birth narrative | Universal | Otto Rank's identified pattern. Hero's birth involves miraculous circumstances, attempt to destroy infant, exposure, rescue and raising by humble or animal foster-parents, eventual recognition.
223 | Trickster Origin Cycle | Universal | Pan-cultural narrative pattern. Trickster figure (Coyote, Anansi, Loki, Maui, Hermes, Eshu) wanders early world breaking rules; byproduct of misadventures is origin of present-world features.
224 | Apocalyptic / End-Times narrative | Universal | Narrative of the foretold or imminent end of an era, world, or order — often involving cosmic battle, judgment, and renewal. Ultimate stakes; structural payoff is reckoning.
225 | Anecdote | Universal | Short narrative of a single incident, usually personal, recounted to make a point, illustrate a character, or generate laughter. The shortest narrative form.
226 | Joke (3-beat) | Universal | Setup, escalation, punchline. Use when laughter is the entire purpose and structural economy is key.
227 | Tall Tale | Universal | Story whose comedy comes from progressive exaggeration — each detail more impossibly large than the last. Often featuring legendary folk heroes (Paul Bunyan, Pecos Bill).
228 | Urban Legend | Universal | Modern folk-narrative presented as having happened to a friend-of-a-friend, typically with horror, ironic, or cautionary twist. Perpetual "almost-true" claim.
229 | Cautionary Tale (general) | Universal | Narrative whose primary function is warning — depicting a character's poor choice and consequences, so audience may avoid the same fate. Moral instruction through negative example.
230 | Riddle-Narrative | Universal | Narrative organized around the posing of a riddle whose solution is the climax. Reader/listener invited to solve along with characters.
231 | Proverb-Narrative | Universal | Short narrative whose closing line is a proverb or saying that crystallizes the tale's meaning. The story exists to ground and authenticate the proverb.
232 | Sermon | Universal | Religious address — typically based on scripture — combining exegesis, moral exhortation, and pastoral application. Interpretive spiritual instruction with explicit application.
233 | Parable | Universal | Short illustrative story in which surface narrative carries spiritual or moral teaching. Distinguished from fable by human characters and religious context.
234 | Prayer | Universal | Direct address to the divine, usually with structured movement: invocation, acknowledgment, petition, thanksgiving, closing. Speaker-to-divine address across traditions.
235 | Confession narrative | Universal | First-person narrative of moral failure and acknowledgment, often within a religious frame seeking forgiveness. Honest accounting before God or community.
236 | Conversion testimony | Universal | First-person narrative of religious conversion. Pre-conversion life, awakening or crisis, decision, transformed post-conversion life.
237 | Hagiography (saint's life) | Universal | Biographical narrative of a holy figure — saint, mystic, religious founder — emphasizing miraculous events, exemplary virtue, and posthumous influence. Devotional register.
238 | Mystical Vision narrative | Universal | First-person account of direct religious experience — vision, ecstasy, union with the divine. Centered on the experience itself, not its conditions.
239 | Eulogy | Universal | Speech delivered in honor of a recently-deceased person, typically at the funeral. Holds collective grief while honoring the individual.
240 | Toast | Universal | Short speech offered at a celebratory gathering, raising a glass in honor of a person, occasion, or shared value. Brevity, public address, ceremonial close.
241 | Wedding Speech (Best Man / Maid of Honor) | Universal | Toast-extension delivered at a wedding by a chosen friend. Mixed tonal register: comedy, sentiment, ceremonial witnessing all in one.
242 | Roast | Universal | Comic ceremonial speech where a person is teased through pointed insults understood as affectionate. The ceremony's structure protects the honoree; affection is underneath the cruelty.
243 | Acceptance Speech | Universal | Speech delivered upon receiving an award or honor. Hybrid: gratitude + brief vision + acknowledgment of others.
244 | Commencement Address | Universal | Speech delivered at graduation ceremony, typically by outside speaker. Engineered for an audience facing transition.
245 | Social Realism | Universal | Fiction depicting working-class life, social conditions, and economic structures, typically with reformist intent. Politically-reformist register.
246 | Naturalism | Universal | Fiction grounded in assumption that human behavior is determined by environment, heredity, and circumstance. Quasi-scientific determinism.
247 | Kitchen-Sink Realism | Universal | Mid-20th-c. British genre depicting working-class domestic life with unflinching ordinariness, typically in industrial-Northern settings.
248 | Dirty Realism | Universal | Late-20th-c. American minimalist fiction depicting the underside of American life — trailer parks, motels, ordinary failures — with stripped-down prose and unsentimental detail.
249 | Greek Tragedy (proper Aristotelian) | Universal | Classical Athenian drama as theorized by Aristotle: noble protagonist's reversal through hamartia, evoking pity and fear, achieving catharsis. Fated structure with choral commentary.
250 | Senecan Tragedy | Universal | Roman tragedy in style of Seneca — five acts, rhetorical heightening, ghosts, on-stage violence, declamation over action. Closet-drama orientation.
