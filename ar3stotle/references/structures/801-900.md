# Structures 801-900

## 801. Re-engagement After Silence
Tradition: Universal (NGC-original)
Beats:
1. Recognition of the time that has passed
2. Specific reason for reaching out now
3. Open invitation to reconnect
Description: 3-beat shape for restarting a dormant relationship — friendship, professional connection, family contact. Acknowledges the gap honestly.
Exemplars: "It's been a while..." messages; LinkedIn reconnection notes; family-reach-out communications.

## 816. Bengali Mangal-Kāvya
Tradition: Indian
Beats:
1. Invocation of Hindu deity (often goddess)
2. Mythic origin of the deity
3. Mortal devotee's encounter
4. Trial through deity's intervention
5. Devotion proven
6. Establishment of cult or worship
Description: Bengali narrative-poem genre (15th-18th c.) celebrating Hindu deities — Manasa (snake goddess), Chandi, Dharmaraj. Established devotional cults through narrative.
Exemplars: Manasa-mangal; Chandi-mangal; Dharma-mangal; Mukundaram's Chandi-mangal.

## 817. Vedic Hymn-Narrative
Tradition: Indian

1. Invocation of deity or cosmic principle
2. Mythic episode recounted
3. Hymn-like praise
4. Petition or affirmation
5. Closing benediction
Description: Sanskrit Vedic hymn form — narrative content embedded in invocational poetry. Foundational South Asian sacred-narrative form.
Exemplars: Rigveda hymns (especially to Indra, Agni, Soma); Atharvaveda; later Upanishadic narrative passages.

## 818. Tamil Bhakti narrative (Tevaram, Tiruvaymoli)

## 819. Hawaiian Mele (chant-narrative)

## 820. Korean Sasol Sijo (long-form sijo)
