# Structures 601-700

## 601. Course Syllabus
Tradition: Universal
Beats:
1. Course title and instructor info
2. Description and learning objectives
3. Required materials
4. Schedule of topics and readings
5. Assignments and grading
6. Policies (attendance, late work, AI use)
Description: Document outlining a course's content, structure, expectations, and policies — distributed to students at term start. Institutional-pedagogical register.
Exemplars: Standard university course syllabi; K-12 course outlines; online-course descriptions.

## 602. Lesson Plan
Tradition: Universal
Beats:
1. Lesson objectives
2. Materials needed
3. Hook or opening
4. Direct instruction
5. Practice or activity
6. Assessment and closure
Description: Detailed instructor's plan for a single class session — objectives, activities, materials, assessment. Specific-session granularity.
Exemplars: Standard K-12 lesson plans; Madeline Hunter lesson plan model; backwards design lesson plans (Wiggins/McTighe).

## 603. Curriculum Design Document
Tradition: Universal
Beats:
1. Educational philosophy and goals
2. Sequence of learning units
3. Skills development progression
4. Assessment plan
5. Resources and texts
6. Implementation timeline
Description: Long-arc document designing an entire course or program of study — sequenced learning, assessment, philosophy. Design-thinking scope.
Exemplars: Common Core curriculum; IB program documents; school-level curriculum mapping; corporate training curricula.

## 604. Reading Comprehension Passage
Tradition: Universal
Beats:
1. Passage with controlled length and difficulty
2. Main idea and supporting details embedded
3. Vocabulary and inference opportunities
4. Followed by question set
Description: Short passage designed for assessment of reading skills — typically followed by questions. Specific testing-instrument register.
Exemplars: SAT, ACT, AP exam passages; standardized testing material; reading curricula.

## 605. Worksheet / Practice Set
Tradition: Universal
Beats:
1. Topic and instructions
2. Worked example or warm-up
3. Easy practice items
4. Progressively harder items
5. Application or word problems
6. Answer key (often)
Description: Set of practice problems or exercises for student practice. Drill-and-practice register and progressive difficulty.
Exemplars: Standard math worksheets; language-learning practice sets; standardized prep books.

## 606. Lab Report
Tradition: Universal
Beats:
1. Title and objective
2. Materials and apparatus
3. Procedure

6. Conclusion
Description: Student's account of a laboratory experiment — protocol, observations, conclusions. Specific empirical-pedagogical register; pre-IMRAD training.
Exemplars: Standard secondary and undergraduate lab reports; chemistry, physics, biology lab notebooks.

## 608. Standardized Test Question
Tradition: Universal
Beats:
1. Stem (question or prompt)
2. Answer choices (if multiple choice)
3. One correct answer; distractors plausible
4. Test-taker's response
Description: Single question on a standardized exam — multiple-choice, short-answer, or essay. Specific assessment-instrument register.
Exemplars: SAT, ACT, GRE, MCAT items; Common Core assessments; psychometric instruments.

## 609. Essay Prompt / Writing Assignment
Tradition: Universal
Beats:

Description: Instructional prompt asking students to write — typically with specific question, length, audience, and criteria. Meta-genre: a prompt that generates a genre.
Exemplars: AP English exam prompts; college admission essay prompts; classroom writing assignments.

## 610. Children's Picture Book
Tradition: Universal

1. Opening illustration introducing protagonist/setting

## 612. Apology Letter
Tradition: Universal
Beats:
1. Acknowledgment of the wrong (specific)
2. Acceptance of responsibility (no excuses)
3. Recognition of impact on the recipient
4. Concrete commitment to change
5. No demand for forgiveness
6. Closing in honest direct voice
Description: Direct written acknowledgment of wrong to a specific person, accepting responsibility and seeking repair. Specific accountability register.
Exemplars: Personal apology letters; corporate apology statements (when honest); literary examples (Augustine to his mother).
